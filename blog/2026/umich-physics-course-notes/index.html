Some Notes on UMich Physics Courses

Created on April 24, 2026

2026   ·   umich   physics   courses   ·   notes

These are just some personal impressions after taking several physics courses at UMich. Your experience may be different, but I hope these notes are useful for students choosing classes.

For students who have experience in the Physics Olympiad, or who went to high school in China and chose physics in the university entrance exam, PHYSICS 390 will probably feel very easy. The homework and exams are mostly repetitions of the lecture slides with a few numbers changed. If you were involved in Olympiad training before, then most 400-level physics courses will also feel quite manageable. A lot of the content still falls well within the scope of Olympiad-style preparation.

For students without that background, my rough feeling about course difficulty is:

405 = 453 > 406 > 401

For exam difficulty, I would rank them roughly as:

401 >= 405 > 453 >= 406

PHYSICS 401 has many different types of questions, and there are often multiple ways to solve the same problem. It helps a lot to be very familiar with the sample exams.

PHYSICS 405 is genuinely difficult, but many students also struggle with it, so a big curve usually comes. In some semesters, even the final may be turned into pure multiple choice.

PHYSICS 453 and PHYSICS 406 are more straightforward. If you stay familiar with the homework style and the main concepts, they are very manageable.

PHYSICS 535 is a really difficult course. The first half is almost a tensor math course, so the first midterm is hard because it feels like a math exam. In the second half, there are many more examples showing how to use those tools in physics, and then it becomes much easier to understand what is going on.

PHYSICS 391 is the best lab course I have ever taken. The GSI and the professor really lead you through the experiment step by step. After each step, they teach you how to use Jupyter Notebook to analyze the data. All of the experiments feel doable. You can always get some result, do some analysis, and show something meaningful at the end.

PHYSICS 441 is a different kind of lab experience. Except for the first lab, you choose three more labs from a long list, and you may be assigned a random lab partner. Each lab lasts three weeks, and there is a final presentation where you can choose any one of those three labs to talk about.

To be honest, some of the labs are not perfectly designed, and some of the instruments are also not in great shape. Because of that, you sometimes spend extra time figuring out how to make the setup work well. But maybe that is also good training for students who want to do research in the future. Overall, it was a great experience: I learned many practical experimental techniques, got better at dealing with unfamiliar situations, and also learned a lot from writing the reports.
